# Behavioral Answer Language

For "Tell me about a time when..." questions. The content structure is **STAR**
(Situation, Task, Action, Result). This file gives you the **English phrases** to
deliver each part naturally, plus the language traps to avoid.

See [../../interview-prep/behavioral-questions.md](../../interview-prep/behavioral-questions.md)
for the question bank and story prep. This file is about *how to say it*.

---

## Opening (frame the story)

- "Sure, a good example of that would be when I was at ___."
- "Yeah, this happened about a year ago, when I was working on ___."
- "The situation that comes to mind is ___."
- "So, some context first: I was on a team that ___."

Keep the setup **short** – 2–3 sentences. Interviewers want the Action and Result.

## Situation / Task

- "At the time, we were ___."
- "The challenge was that ___."
- "My role was to ___." / "I was responsible for ___."
- "We had a deadline of ___ and ___."
- "The tricky part was ___."

## Action (the core – spend most of your time here, use "I" not "we")

- "So the first thing I did was ___."
- "I started by ___ing."
- "I decided to ___ because ___."
- "I reached out to ___ to ___."
- "Rather than ___, I chose to ___."
- "I took the lead on ___."
- "I made sure to ___."
- "Once ___, I moved on to ___."
- "There was some pushback, so I ___."

⚠️ **Say "I", not "we", for your actions.** "We fixed it" tells the interviewer
nothing about *you*. "I proposed X, then I implemented Y, and I coordinated with Z."

## Result (quantify if you can)

- "As a result, ___."
- "In the end, we managed to ___."
- "That brought the ___ down from ___ to ___."
- "It ended up ___."
- "The feedback was ___."
- "Since then, ___."

## Reflection (what you learned – shows maturity)

- "What I took away from that was ___."
- "Looking back, I'd probably ___ differently – I'd ___."
- "It taught me the importance of ___."
- "Now I always ___ because of that experience."

---

## Tense discipline

The whole story is in the **past**: "I noticed... I decided... we shipped..."
Only switch to present for lasting lessons: "So now I always write tests first."
Don't drift into present tense mid-story ("So I go to my manager and I say...") –
that's informal storytelling style and can sound off in an interview.

---

## Phrases for specific common questions

**A conflict with a colleague:**
- "We had a genuine disagreement about ___."
- "I tried to understand where they were coming from."
- "I suggested we ___ to settle it with data rather than opinion."
- "We ended up ___, and honestly the working relationship got better after that."

**A failure / mistake:**
- "I'll be honest – this one didn't go well."
- "In hindsight, I underestimated ___."
- "As soon as I realized, I ___."
- "I owned it with the team and we ___."
- "The main lesson was ___."

**A tight deadline / pressure:**
- "Things got pretty intense when ___."
- "I had to prioritise hard – I focused on ___ and pushed back on ___."
- "I kept the stakeholders updated so there were no surprises."

**Leadership / influence without authority:**
- "I wasn't the lead, but I ___."
- "I got buy-in by ___."
- "I brought people along by ___."

**Handling ambiguity:**
- "The requirements were pretty vague, so I ___."
- "I made some assumptions and validated them early with ___."

---

## Fillers to avoid over-using here

In behavioral answers, "like", "you know", "basically", "kind of" can make you
sound unsure about your *own experience*. A few are fine; monitor it in recordings.
Replace with a short pause.

---

## Drill (Weeks 7–12)

1. Prep 6–8 stories (from the behavioral-questions file).
2. 2×/week: pick one, record a 2-minute answer using the phrases above.
3. Listen back and score: Was it STAR? Did I say "I"? Past tense throughout? A
   number in the result? A reflection at the end? Under 2.5 min?
4. Re-record the weak ones. By Week 12 your top 6 stories should be smooth and
   roughly memorized in *structure* (not word-for-word).
