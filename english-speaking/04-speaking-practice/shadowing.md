# Shadowing

Shadowing = listening to a recording of a native speaker and speaking along with it,
almost simultaneously (about half a second behind), copying **everything** –
pronunciation, stress, rhythm, intonation, speed, pauses.

It's the highest-leverage drill for spoken English because it trains all layers at
once and forces your mouth to move at native speed. 10 minutes of daily shadowing
for 3 months visibly changes how you sound.

---

## What you need

- **Audio of natural speech** you enjoy: a podcast, a YouTube video, a TV/movie
  scene, an audiobook. Conversational, not a news anchor reading.
- **A transcript / subtitles** (needed at the start, optional later).
- **Earphones** – you need to hear the original clearly while you talk.
- Your voice recorder for checking.

Good sources: see [../06-listening/resources.md](../06-listening/resources.md).
Pick ONE main source and stick with it for weeks – familiarity compounds.

---

## The method

### Stage 0 – Choose a clip (30–60 seconds)
Short. One clip, reused for 2–3 days until smooth, then a new one.

### Stage 1 – Understand it (2 min)
Read the transcript. Look up any unknown words. You must know what every sentence
means before you shadow it.

### Stage 2 – Listen and mark (2 min)
Play it 2×. Mark on the transcript: stressed words (underline), pauses (`/`), pitch
jumps (↑↓), and any linking/reductions you hear ("gonna", "kinda", words blending).
→ notation from [../01-pronunciation/connected-speech.md](../01-pronunciation/connected-speech.md).

### Stage 3 – Slow shadowing, with text (3 min)
Play the audio. Speak along, reading the transcript, staying ~0.5s behind. Don't
pause the audio – if you fall behind, skip ahead and rejoin. Do it 3–4 times.
Goal: match the **rhythm and stress**, not just the words.

### Stage 4 – Full-speed shadowing, with text (3 min)
Same, but now really chase the speed and melody. Exaggerate the intonation. 3–4
passes.

### Stage 5 – Shadowing without text (2 min)
Put the transcript away. Shadow from memory + ear. It's fine to miss bits.

### Stage 6 – Check (once, when you switch clips)
Record yourself shadowing. Play original, then yours. Note the 3 biggest gaps
(a sound? the speed? going flat? a specific word?). Those become drill targets.

---

## Two variants

- **Synchronized shadowing** (above): speak *at the same time* as the audio. Best
  for rhythm/speed.
- **Echo shadowing**: listen to a phrase, pause, repeat it exactly, then continue.
  Best for accuracy and memory. Use this when a clip is hard.

Alternate them. Hard clip → echo. Familiar clip → synchronized.

---

## Common mistakes

- **Reading, not copying.** You're reading the transcript in your own accent,
  ignoring the audio. Fix: focus on the *sound*, use the text only as a safety net.
- **Pausing the audio constantly.** Kills the rhythm training. Let it run.
- **Clip too long or too hard.** 30–60s, ~90% comprehensible.
- **Monotone.** You're saying the words but not the *melody*. Exaggerate pitch.
- **New clip every day.** Repetition is where the gains are. 2–3 days per clip.
- **Silent mouthing.** Full voice, or it doesn't count.

---

## Progression

| Weeks | Clip type | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Slow, clear conversational podcast | Just keeping up; word stress |
| 3–4 | Same, slightly faster | Sentence rhythm; reductions |
| 5–8 | TV show dialogue (natural speed) | Intonation; connected speech |
| 9–12 | Any content at full speed, minimal text | Match speed and melody freely |

---

## Micro-shadowing (when you have 2 minutes)

Take **one** sentence you like. Shadow it 15–20 times until it's effortless and
sounds just like the original. Add it to your "sentence bank" – you now own that
rhythm pattern and can reuse it. Do this with 1–2 sentences a day and you build a
library of native-sounding phrases.
