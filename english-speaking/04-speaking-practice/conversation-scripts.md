# Conversation Scripts

Realistic dialogues for practice. Use them **three ways**:

1. **Shadow both roles** – read along with... wait, there's no audio. Instead:
   read each line aloud with full intonation, playing both people.
2. **Half-dialogue** – cover one side. You improvise that side; the visible side is
   your cue. Record it.
3. **With a partner / tutor** – act it out, then go off-script and keep the
   conversation going.

After each: note phrases you'd struggle to produce, and add them to Anki.

The **bold phrases** are high-frequency conversational moves worth memorizing.

---

## 1. Small talk with a colleague (kitchen / before a meeting)

- A: Hey, how's it going?
- B: **Not bad, thanks. Bit of a slow morning.** You?
- A: Yeah, same. **I haven't really got going yet.** How was your weekend?
- B: It was good, pretty quiet. **We just stayed in mostly.** You did something, right?
- A: Yeah, we went hiking on Saturday. **It was nice to get out of the house.**
- B: Sounds good. **The weather held up?**
- A: Mostly. It drizzled a bit at the end but nothing serious.
- B: Nice. Anyway, **I should get back to it.** Catch you later.
- A: **Yeah, talk soon.**

## 2. Joining a video call

- A: Hi everyone. **Can you hear me okay?**
- B: **Yep, coming through fine.**
- A: Great. **Sorry, give me one sec** – just closing a few tabs.
- B: No worries. **Shall we wait for Priya or get started?**
- A: **Let's give her a minute.** ... Okay, I think we can start. **Do you want to
  kick us off?**
- B: Sure. So the main thing I wanted to cover today is...

## 3. Daily standup update

- **Yesterday I** finished the login page and **got it reviewed.**
- **Today I'm going to** start on the password reset flow.
- **I'm blocked on** one thing – I need access to the staging database. **Could
  someone help me with that after this?**
- Other than that, **I'm on track for** the Friday deadline.

(Practice: give your real standup update out loud every day for a week.)

## 4. Asking for help

- A: Hey, **have you got a minute?**
- B: Sure, what's up?
- A: **I'm a bit stuck on** this deployment error. **I've tried** restarting the
  service and clearing the cache, but **no luck.** **Do you have any idea what
  might be going on?**
- B: Hmm. **Have you checked** the environment variables?
- A: **I think so, but let me double-check.** ... Actually, you're right, one's
  missing. **That would explain it.** Thanks, **you just saved me an hour.**
- B: **No problem. Let me know if it's still broken.**

## 5. Disagreeing politely in a meeting

- A: I think we should rewrite the whole module.
- B: **I see where you're coming from,** but **I'm not sure that's the best use of
  our time right now.** **The way I see it,** we could fix the main bug in a day and
  revisit the rewrite next quarter.
- A: But the code is really hard to maintain.
- B: **That's a fair point.** **What if we** do the quick fix now and **I'll write
  up** a proposal for the rewrite so we can plan it properly?
- A: **Yeah, that works for me.**

## 6. Catching up with an old friend

- A: It's been ages! **How have you been?**
- B: **Yeah, way too long.** I've been good – busy, but good. **I actually changed
  jobs** a few months ago.
- A: Oh nice, **how's that going?**
- B: **So far so good.** **It took a while to settle in,** but I like the team. What
  about you? **Still at the same place?**
- A: Yeah, **still there.** **Nothing dramatic** – same role, bit more
  responsibility. **We should do this more often.**
- B: **Definitely. Let's not leave it another year.**

## 7. At a restaurant

- Waiter: Are you ready to order, or **do you need a few more minutes?**
- You: **I think we're ready.** **Could I get** the grilled chicken, please?
- Waiter: Sure. **Any sides with that?**
- You: **What would you recommend?**
- Waiter: The roasted vegetables are really popular.
- You: **I'll go with that then.** And **could we get** some more water **when you
  get a chance?**
- Waiter: Of course. **I'll be right back with that.**

## 8. Phone call – something went wrong

- A: Hi, I'm calling about **an issue with my order.**
- B: **Sorry to hear that. Can I take your order number?**
- A: Yeah, it's 4471. **The thing is,** I ordered two items but only one arrived.
- B: **Let me have a look.** ... **You're right,** the second item was **out of
  stock.** **I can either** refund you **or** send it once it's back in stock.
- A: **I'd rather** just get the refund, thanks.
- B: **No problem. That's all sorted** – you'll see it in 3–5 days.

## 9. Job interview – opening

- I: **Thanks for coming in.** **To start, could you** tell me a bit about yourself?
- You: **Sure.** **So, I'm** a backend developer with **around five years of
  experience,** mostly in fintech. **I'm currently at** [company], **where I** work
  on payment systems. **Before that** I was at a smaller startup, **which is where
  I** really learned to own projects end to end. **What drew me to this role is**
  the scale of the problems you're working on.

## 10. Explaining a technical concept to a non-dev

- A: So what does your team actually do?
- B: **Okay, so you know how** when you buy something online, the payment "just
  works"? **My team builds the part that** makes sure the money actually moves from
  your bank to the shop's bank, safely. **Think of it like** a really careful
  middleman. **The tricky part is** doing it fast, and never losing track of a
  payment, even if something crashes halfway.
- A: Huh, I never thought about that.
- B: **Yeah, most people don't – which means we're doing our job.**
