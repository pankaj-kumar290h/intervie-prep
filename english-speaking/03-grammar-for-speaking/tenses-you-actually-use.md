# Tenses You Actually Use

You need **6** tenses fluently. That's it. Master these and you can say almost
anything in conversation.

For each: when to use it, the form, a model sentence to drill, and the common
Hindi-speaker error.

---

## 1. Present Simple – facts, habits, routines, permanent situations

**Form:** I/you/we/they **work** · he/she/it **works**
**Use:** "I **work** at a fintech company." · "She **lives** in Pune." · "The train
**leaves** at 6." · "I usually **wake up** at 7."

**Errors to kill:**
- Missing `-s`: "He **work** here" → "He **works** here."
- Using continuous for states: "I **am knowing** him" → "I **know** him." Also:
  *want, need, like, love, hate, believe, understand, remember, mean, own, belong,
  seem, prefer* – normally NOT continuous.
- "I **am having** a car" → "I **have** a car." (But "I'm having lunch" = action, OK.)

**Drill:** Say 10 sentences about your routine and job, all present simple, all with
correct `-s` on he/she/it.

---

## 2. Present Continuous – happening now, or temporary / around now

**Form:** am/is/are + verb-ing
**Use:** "I**'m working** on a new feature this week." · "What **are you doing**?" ·
"He**'s** not **coming** today." · "Prices **are rising**."

**Errors:**
- Question word order: "What you are doing?" → "What **are you** doing?"
- Using it for permanent facts: "I **am living** in India" (if permanent) →
  "I **live** in India." Use continuous only if temporary: "I**'m living** with my
  parents *for now*."

**Drill:** Describe what's going on in your life/work "these days" – 8 sentences.

---

## 3. Past Simple – finished actions at a finished time

**Form:** regular +ed · irregular (went, saw, did, had, made, took, got, said...)
**Use:** "I **joined** the company in 2022." · "Yesterday I **fixed** the bug." ·
"We **launched** last month." · "**Did** you **see** the email?"

**Errors:**
- Present perfect instead: "I **have joined** in 2022" → "I **joined** in 2022."
  (With a finished time word — yesterday, last week, in 2022, ago, then — use PAST
  SIMPLE, never present perfect.)
- "I **did** not **went**" → "I **did**n't **go**." (After *did*, use base verb.)
- Irregular verbs: learn your top 50 cold (go/went, come/came, take/took, buy/bought,
  bring/brought, think/thought, teach/taught, catch/caught, build/built, send/sent,
  spend/spent, lose/lost, win/won, find/found, feel/felt, keep/kept, leave/left,
  meet/met, pay/paid, run/ran, sit/sat, speak/spoke, write/wrote, break/broke,
  choose/chose, drive/drove, forget/forgot, give/gave, know/knew, see/saw, show/showed,
  understand/understood, become/became, begin/began, hold/held, mean/meant, read/read).

**Drill:** Tell the story of your last work week in past simple. 12 sentences.

---

## 4. Present Perfect – past action, **unfinished time** or **result matters now**

**Form:** have/has + past participle (gone, seen, done, been, made, taken, written...)
**Use:**
- Life experience (time not stated): "I**'ve worked** with React before." · "**Have
  you ever** used Kubernetes?"
- Unfinished time: "I**'ve** had three meetings **today**." (today isn't over)
- Result now: "I**'ve finished** the report." (so it's ready now) · "She**'s** just
  **left**." · "I**'ve lost** my keys." (I don't have them now)
- With *for / since* for something continuing: "I**'ve worked** here **for** two
  years." / "**since** 2022."

**Errors (the big one for Hindi speakers):**
- Using it with a finished time: "I **have met** him **yesterday**" ❌ →
  "I **met** him yesterday." ✅
- **since vs for:** *for* + duration (for 3 years, for 2 hours, for a long time).
  *since* + starting point (since 2022, since Monday, since I joined). Never "since 3
  years."
- Using present simple for duration: "I **work** here since 2 years" ❌ →
  "I**'ve been working** here **for** 2 years." ✅

**Drill:** 8 sentences: things you've done in your career (no dates), + 3 with
for/since.

---

## 5. Present Perfect Continuous – emphasis on the ongoing activity / recent activity

**Form:** have/has been + verb-ing
**Use:** "I**'ve been working** on this bug all morning." · "How long **have you
been learning** English?" · "She**'s been feeling** tired lately." · "It**'s been
raining** all day."

Difference from present perfect: PPC stresses the *activity/duration*; PP stresses
the *result/completion*. "I**'ve painted** the room" (done) vs "I**'ve been
painting** the room" (that's why I'm covered in paint; maybe not done).

**Drill:** "How long have you been...?" answer 5 versions (learning English, working
here, living in your city, using X tool, doing Y hobby).

---

## 6. Future – 3 ways, pick the right one

- **going to** – plans/intentions + predictions from evidence:
  "I**'m going to** learn AWS this year." · "Look at those clouds – it**'s going to**
  rain."
- **will** – decisions made now, offers, promises, predictions/opinions:
  "I**'ll** send it now." · "I think it**'ll** be fine." · "**I'll** help you."
- **present continuous** – fixed arrangements (with a time/place):
  "I**'m meeting** the client at 3." · "We**'re flying** to Delhi on Monday."

**Errors:**
- "I **will go** to a party tomorrow" (already arranged) → "I**'m going to** a party"
  / "I**'m going to go**."
- After *if / when / as soon as / before / after / until* → use present, not will:
  "When I **will get** home..." ❌ → "When I **get** home, I**'ll** call you." ✅

**Drill:** 3 sentences with *going to* (plans), 3 with *will* (spontaneous
decisions), 3 with present continuous (arrangements).

---

## Also useful (learn after the 6)

- **used to** – past habit that stopped: "I **used to** commute two hours." (Not
  present. For present habit: "I usually...")
- **would** (past repeated action, storytelling): "Every summer we**'d** go to my
  village."
- **Modals:** can/could (ability, requests), should/ought to (advice), have to/must
  (obligation), might/may (possibility), would (hypothetical/polite).
- **First conditional:** "If it **works**, we**'ll** ship it."
- **Second conditional:** "If I **had** more time, I**'d** refactor it." (hypothetical)
- **Passive:** "The bug **was fixed** yesterday." · "This **is handled** by the
  gateway." (common in technical explanation)

## Weekly focus (Weeks 2–5)

| Week | Tense focus | Self-talk prompt |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Present simple + continuous | "My job and my current week" |
| 3 | Past simple | "What I did yesterday / last weekend" |
| 4 | Present perfect + for/since | "My experience and how long" |
| 5 | Future (3 forms) + conditionals | "My plans and what I'd do if..." |

Drill each by substituting your life into the model sentences, out loud, daily.
