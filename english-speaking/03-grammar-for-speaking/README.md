# 03 – Grammar for Speaking

You almost certainly know more grammar than you can *use in real time*. This folder
is **not** a grammar course. It's the small set of grammar you need to speak
smoothly, drilled until it's automatic.

## Principle: automate a few patterns, don't study everything

When speaking, you have no time to think about rules. You need ~15 sentence frames
and ~5 tense contrasts to be **reflexes**. Everything else can stay imperfect for
now – listeners forgive tense slips; they don't forgive long silent pauses while you
conjugate.

## How to practice grammar for speaking

1. Learn the pattern with **one** clear model sentence.
2. Substitute your own content into the frame, out loud, 10×.
3. Force the pattern into your daily self-talk that day.
4. Catch violations in your recordings → add to the error log in
   [../progress-tracker.md](../progress-tracker.md).
5. Spend one week per error, then move on. Don't try to fix everything at once.

## The 20% that causes 80% of errors (for Hindi speakers)

- **Present perfect vs simple past** – "I have gone yesterday" ❌ → "I went yesterday" ✅
- **"since" vs "for"** – "since 3 years" ❌ → "for 3 years" ✅
- **Articles** – dropping "the"/"a" or adding them where not needed
- **Continuous overuse** – "I am knowing", "I am having a car" ❌
- **Prepositions** – "discuss about", "reach to", "married with"
- **"-s" on 3rd person** – "he work here" ❌ → "he works here" ✅
- **Question formation** – "What you are doing?" ❌ → "What are you doing?" ✅
- **Reported speech word order** – "I don't know what is the time" ❌ → "...what the time is" ✅

Each of these has a section in the files here.
