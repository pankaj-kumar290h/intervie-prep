# Spoken Sentence Patterns

Fluent speakers reuse a small set of **sentence frames** constantly. If these are
automatic, you start a sentence confidently and your brain fills the blank while
your mouth is already moving. Memorize these like vocabulary.

Drill method: for each frame, say it 10× with different content about your life.

---

## Starting / buying time

- "The thing is, ___."
- "What happened was, ___."
- "Basically, ___."
- "To be honest, ___."
- "The way I see it, ___."
- "Here's the thing: ___."
- "So what I'm trying to say is ___."
- "Let me put it this way: ___."

## Softening / being polite (very useful at work)

- "I was wondering if you could ___." ("...send me the file.")
- "Would it be possible to ___?"
- "Do you think you could ___?"
- "I don't mean to be a pain, but ___."
- "Sorry to bother you, but ___."
- "If it's not too much trouble, ___."
- "Just to check – ___?"
- "Would you mind ___ing?" ("...taking a look?")
- "It might be worth ___ing." ("...trying a different approach.")

## Giving opinions

- "If you ask me, ___."
- "I'd say ___."
- "It seems to me that ___."
- "I get the feeling that ___."
- "As far as I'm concerned, ___."
- "I'm not 100% sure, but I think ___."
- "I could be wrong, but ___."
- "From my experience, ___."

## Agreeing / disagreeing

- "That's a good point."
- "I see what you mean, but ___."
- "I'm with you on that."
- "Fair enough, though I still think ___."
- "I'm not so sure about that."
- "I get where you're coming from, but ___."
- "Exactly – that's what I was thinking."
- "I'd have to disagree there."

## Explaining / clarifying

- "What I mean is ___."
- "In other words, ___."
- "Let me rephrase that."
- "The point I'm making is ___."
- "To give you an example, ___."
- "It's kind of like ___." (analogy)
- "Think of it as ___."
- "The difference is ___."

## Comparing / contrasting

- "Compared to ___, this is ___."
- "The main difference is ___."
- "On the one hand ___, on the other hand ___."
- "It's similar to ___, except ___."
- "Whereas ___, this one ___."

## Describing cause and effect

- "The reason (why) ___ is that ___."
- "That's why ___."
- "As a result, ___."
- "Because of that, ___."
- "This led to ___."
- "It ended up ___ing."

## Talking about the past (storytelling)

- "So, a while back, ___."
- "This was when I was ___ing."
- "At first ___, but then ___."
- "Long story short, ___."
- "It turned out that ___."
- "In the end, ___."

## Hypothetical / advice

- "If I were you, I'd ___."
- "If that happened, I'd probably ___."
- "What I'd do is ___."
- "You could always ___."
- "It might be better to ___."
- "In an ideal world, ___."

## Not knowing / being vague

- "I'm not sure how to put this."
- "I can't think of the word, but it's like ___."
- "It's something to do with ___."
- "You know, that thing where ___."
- "Off the top of my head, ___."
- "Don't quote me on this, but ___."

## Indirect questions (fixes word-order errors)

Direct: "Where **is** the file?" → Indirect: "Do you know where the file **is**?"
The verb goes **after** the subject in the second half; no "do/does/did".

- "Could you tell me where ___ is?"
- "Do you know when ___?"
- "I was wondering what ___."
- "I'm not sure why ___."
- "Can I ask how ___?"

❌ "I don't know what is the problem." → ✅ "I don't know what the problem **is**."
❌ "Tell me where do you live." → ✅ "Tell me where you **live**."

## Reacting in conversation (keep it alive)

- "Oh really? How come?"
- "That makes sense."
- "Wait, so you mean ___?"
- "No way!"
- "Same here."
- "That must have been ___." ("...tough.")
- "And then what happened?"
- "I know, right?"

## Weekly drill (Week 5)

- Mon: starting + buying time
- Tue: softening / polite requests (record yourself making 5 work requests)
- Wed: opinions + agree/disagree
- Thu: explaining / clarifying / comparing
- Fri: storytelling + indirect questions
- Sat: mixed – have a 3-minute monologue and consciously use 8 different frames

Keep a printed list of your 15 favorites near your desk for a month.
