# Recovering from Mistakes

Everyone makes mistakes while speaking – natives included. What separates confident
speakers from stuck ones is **how they recover**. A smooth recovery is nearly
invisible; a panic freeze is what people actually notice.

---

## Rule 1: Most mistakes – just keep going

If you say a wrong tense or a slightly off word but the meaning is clear, **do not
stop**. Stopping to fix a minor error:
- breaks your flow and the listener's attention,
- makes a non-issue into a visible issue,
- eats the mental energy you need for the next sentence.

"Yesterday I have gone to..." — listener understood "yesterday I went". Move on.
Note it later if it's a pattern.

## Rule 2: Fix it only if meaning is broken – and do it smoothly

If the error actually changes or blocks the meaning, self-correct with a standard
phrase (natives use these constantly):

- "...sorry, I mean ___."
- "...or rather, ___."
- "...well, not exactly – what I mean is ___."
- "Let me put that differently."
- "Sorry, let me start that sentence again."

Said calmly, these sound *competent*, not weak.

## Rule 3: Lost the sentence halfway? Abandon and restart

If you've tangled the grammar so badly you can't finish:

- "Hmm, let me rephrase that."
- "Okay, starting over – ___."
- "Sorry, that came out wrong. Basically, ___."

Then say a **simpler** version. Don't try to rescue the complicated sentence.

## Rule 4: Can't find a word? Talk around it (circumlocution)

This is a core fluency skill – practice it on purpose.

- "the thing you use to ___"
- "it's like a ___ but ___"
- "the opposite of ___"
- "when you ___, that's called... anyway, when you do that"
- "I forget the word – it's basically ___"

Then continue. Look the word up afterward and Anki it.

## Rule 5: Didn't understand them? Ask – it's normal

Never nod and pretend. Clarifying is a *strength*.

- "Sorry, could you say that again?"
- "I didn't quite catch that."
- "Do you mean ___?"
- "Sorry, what do you mean by ___?"
- "Could you rephrase that?"
- "Just to make sure I understood – you're saying ___?"
- "Sorry, you're going a bit fast for me – could you slow down slightly?"

## Rule 6: Wrong word came out and you got a confused look

- "Sorry, that's not the word I wanted. I mean ___."
- "Ignore that – what I meant was ___."
- Laugh it off: "That's not English, is it? Let me try again."

A little humor about your own slip makes everyone relax, including you.

---

## After the conversation: the real fixing

The moment to fix errors is **not** while speaking – it's after.

1. If you recorded it: listen once. Note recurring error **patterns** (not every
   slip). "I keep saying 'since' for durations." "I drop 'the' before job titles."
2. Put each pattern in the error log in
   [../progress-tracker.md](../progress-tracker.md).
3. Drill that ONE pattern for a week (correct sentences, out loud, in context).
4. Then it mostly self-corrects and you move to the next.

Trying to fix everything at once fixes nothing. One pattern per week.

---

## The confidence loop

Freeze at a mistake → panic → more mistakes → avoid speaking → stay stuck.
Recover smoothly → keep talking → get your point across → feel capable → speak more
→ improve.
