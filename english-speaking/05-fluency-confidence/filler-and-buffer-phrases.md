# Filler and Buffer Phrases

When you need a moment to think, native speakers don't go silent – they use a
**buffer phrase**. It keeps you talking, sounds natural, and buys you 2–3 seconds to
find the next word. Memorize a handful from each group so they come out
automatically.

> ⚠️ Fillers are seasoning, not the meal. Overusing "you know" or "like" every 3
> words is a habit to avoid. But a few, well-placed, are 100% natural and far better
> than dead silence or "aaaa...".

---

## Starting / opening a thought

- "Well..."
- "So..."
- "Right, so..."
- "Okay, so basically..."
- "The thing is..."
- "Let me think for a second."
- "That's a good question."
- "Hmm, how do I put this..."
- "Where do I start..."

## Buying time in the middle

- "..., you know, ..."
- "..., I mean, ..."
- "..., sort of, ..." / "..., kind of, ..."
- "..., like, ..." (informal, use sparingly)
- "..., how can I say, ..."
- "..., what's the word, ..."
- "..., let me see, ..."
- "..., basically, ..."
- "..., if that makes sense, ..."

## When you can't find a word

- "It's like a... you know, the thing you use for..."
- "I can't think of the exact word, but it's basically ___."
- "There's a word for this... anyway, it means ___."
- "How do you say... the thing that ___."
- "Something like ___." / "___, or something like that."
- (then just describe it and move on – don't stop)

## When you need to restart the sentence

- "Sorry, let me start again."
- "What I'm trying to say is ___."
- "Let me rephrase that."
- "Actually, hang on – ___."
- "Or rather, ___."

## Softening before you answer

- "To be honest, ___."
- "Off the top of my head, ___."
- "I'd say ___."
- "It depends, but ___."
- "I'm not totally sure, but ___."

## Agreeing / reacting while you think of your point

- "Yeah, exactly."
- "That's true."
- "Right, right."
- "I see what you mean."
- "Good point. And I guess ___."
- "Interesting. So ___."

## Checking the listener is with you

- "Does that make sense?"
- "You know what I mean?"
- "If that makes sense?"
- "Right?"

## Closing a thought

- "..., so yeah."
- "..., that kind of thing."
- "..., anyway."
- "..., if that answers your question."
- "So that's basically it."

---

## The "hesitation sounds"

English uses **"uh"** and **"um"** (and "er", "erm" in the UK) – not the Hindi "aa"
or a long silence. It's a small thing but it makes hesitation sound native. A short
"um" while you think reads as normal; a 4-second silence reads as "he's lost."

---

## Drill (Week 3)

1. Pick **10** buffer phrases (2 from each group). Write them on a card.
2. Say each 10× until automatic.
3. In your daily self-talk, **deliberately over-use** them for one week – aim for
   one buffer phrase every 15–20 seconds. It'll feel excessive; that's the point of
   the drill. It'll settle to a natural rate.
4. In recordings, check: when you got stuck, did you buffer or did you go silent?
   Silent = drill more.

## Target

By Week 6: you never have a silent freeze longer than ~1 second. You always have a
phrase to keep the floor while your brain catches up.
