# Consonant Sounds

Most English consonants exist in Hindi. Focus on the ~7 that are missing, swapped,
or positioned differently.

---

## 1. `th` – two sounds (see also common-mistakes file)

- Voiceless `θ`: `think, thanks, three, month, both, healthy, nothing`.
- Voiced `ð`: `this, that, the, they, mother, other, breathe, rhythm`.
- Tongue tip lightly touches the bottom of the top teeth (or peeks between teeth).
  Air flows over it. Do **not** substitute `t/d/s/z`.
- Test word full of it: "**Th**e **th**ree bro**th**ers **th**ink **th**at ba**th** is heal**th**y."

---

## 2. `v` vs `w` (see common-mistakes file)

- `v`: teeth on lip, buzz. `w`: rounded lips, no teeth.
- "**W**e **v**oted for the **v**ery **w**orst **w**ine."

---

## 3. Light `l` vs dark `l`

- **Light `l`** – before a vowel: `light, love, believe, alive`. Tongue tip up front.
- **Dark `l`** – after a vowel / end of word: `full, ball, milk, people, cool, well`.
  Back of the tongue lifts; sounds almost like "w/oo". Hindi speakers often use light
  `l` everywhere, so `ball` sounds too crisp.
- "The **l**itt"e gir**l** fe**ll** off the ta**ll** wa**ll**."

---

## 4. `z` and voiced `-se`

`z` = buzzing `s`. `zoo, zero, zip, lazy, busy, easy`. And spelled with `s`:
`is, was, has, does, these, those, please, because, choose, rise, noise, cars, jobs`.
"Tho**s**e bu**s**y bee**s** buzz becau**s**e it'**s** ea**s**y."

---

## 5. `s` clusters at word start – no vowel before

`speak, student, school, street, sport, small, snake, scream, spring`.
Go straight from `s` into the next consonant. Practice: hold a long "sssss" then add
the rest: "sssss-peak", "sssss-treet", shortening the `s` each rep.

---

## 6. `t` and `d` – alveolar, not retroflex

Tongue tip on the ridge behind the top teeth, not curled back. Lighter than Hindi
ट/ड. `time, table, today, department, understand`.
US "flap": between vowels, `t` → quick `d`-tap: `water, better, later, computer,
data, matter, city, party, thirty`. Try: "I got a **b**e**tt**er **compu**ter for
**th**ir**ty** dollars."

---

## 7. `-ng` at the end

`sing, ring, running, going, thing, morning`. It's a single nasal sound `ŋ`, **not**
`n` + hard `g`. Don't say "sing-g" or "runn-ing-g". The back of the tongue lifts;
the sound comes through the nose; no `g` pop at the end (in most accents).

---

## 8. `p`, `t`, `k` – aspiration at the start of stressed syllables

In English, `p/t/k` at the start of a stressed syllable have a puff of air:
`pin, top, come`. Hold your hand in front of your mouth – you should feel the puff.
Hindi has both aspirated and unaspirated stops, so this is usually easy for you, but
watch that you're not using the unaspirated version (`p` sounding like `b`).

After `s` they are **not** aspirated: `spin, stop, skate` (the `p/t/k` sound almost
like `b/d/g`).

---

## 9. `j` vs `z` vs `y`

- `j` (`jump, job, age, manager, page`) = `d` + `zh`. A stop then a buzz.
- `y` (`yes, year, young, use, yellow`) = a glide, tongue high, no friction.
- Don't swap: `year` ≠ "jeer", `use` (verb, "yooz") ≠ "jooz".

---

## 10. Silent letters (say the word, not the spelling)

`know, knee` (no k) · `write, wrong` (no w) · `hour, honest` (no h) · `island,
aisle` (no s) · `debt, doubt, subtle` (no b) · `castle, listen, whistle` (no t) ·
`comb, thumb, climb` (no b) · `receipt` (no p) · `Wednesday` ("wenz-day") ·
`half, calf, walk, talk, could, should` (no l).

---

## Drill routine (Week 3, 5 min/day)

1. One target sound.
2. 10 words with it – recorded, checked on YouGlish (2 min).
3. One loaded sentence, 10× (2 min):
   - `th`: "I think that these things are worth thirty thousand."
   - `v/w`: "We were very worried when the white van wouldn't move."
   - dark `l`: "Bill will fill the small bottle with cold milk."
   - `ŋ`: "I'm going running in the morning; nothing is wrong."
4. Then read one paragraph aloud focusing only on that sound (1 min).
