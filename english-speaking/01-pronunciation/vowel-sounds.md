# Vowel Sounds

English has ~15–20 distinct vowel sounds (depends on accent) written with only 5–6
letters. Hindi speakers often merge them into a smaller set. You don't need IPA
mastery – you need to **distinguish the pairs that cause misunderstandings**.

---

## The short vs long pairs (drill these first)

| Short | Long | Difference | Practice sentence |
|-------|------|-----------|-------------------|
| `ɪ` – ship, bit, live, fill | `iː` – sheep, beat, leave, feel | long one: lips spread wide, held longer | "This ship will leave the sea." |
| `ʊ` – full, pull, foot, could | `uː` – fool, pool, food, cooed | long one: lips tight and rounded, held | "Don't be a fool, the pool is full." |
| `ɒ`/`ʌ` – cot, cup, luck, but | `ɔː` – caught, cord, walk, born | long one: jaw drops, lips round | "He caught a cold in the cot." |
| `æ` – cat, bad, man, hat | `ɑː` – cart, bard, calm, heart (UK) | `æ` is bright/front; `ɑː` is dark/back | "The cat sat in the cart." |
| `e` – bed, said, men | `eɪ` – bade, shade, main | `eɪ` glides from e→i | "They said it was made." |

**How to train a pair:**
1. Listen to 10 minimal pairs on YouGlish / a minimal-pairs site. Just listen.
2. Have someone (or a TTS) say one at random – you point to which it was.
3. Now you say them; record; check.
4. Put both in one sentence and say it 10×.

---

## The `æ` sound (cat, man, apple, back, have)

Often replaced with a Hindi "ऍ" that's too closed, so `bad` sounds like `bed` or
`bud`. Real `æ`: open your mouth wider than feels natural, pull the corners of your
lips slightly back, tongue low and forward. `cat, bad, happy, family, tax, plan`.

---

## The schwa `ə` – the most common sound in English

Every unstressed syllable tends to collapse to a lazy "uh": `ə`.

- `about` = "ə-BOUT"
- `banana` = "bə-NA-nə"
- `problem` = "PROB-ləm"
- `support` = "sə-PORT"
- `the` (before consonant) = "thə"
- `to`, `of`, `for`, `and`, `a`, `can`, `was`, `at` → almost always "tə, əv, fər, ən, ə, kən, wəz, ət" in normal speech.

If you pronounce every vowel "fully" and clearly, you sound robotic and slow.
**Reducing unstressed vowels to schwa is what makes you sound fluent.**
→ see [word-stress-and-rhythm.md](word-stress-and-rhythm.md) and
[connected-speech.md](connected-speech.md).

## `r`-colored vowels (US accent)

In US English the `r` colors the vowel: `car, bird, four, here, hair, sure, teacher`.
The tongue bunches back. In UK English the `r` is silent at the end / before a
consonant (`car` = "cah"). **Pick one accent and be consistent.**

## Diphthongs (two vowels glued together – one glide)

| Sound | Examples |
|-------|----------|
| `eɪ` | day, make, rain, they, eight |
| `aɪ` | my, time, high, buy, eye |
| `ɔɪ` | boy, coin, noise, enjoy |
| `aʊ` | now, house, out, down |
| `oʊ` (US) / `əʊ` (UK) | go, home, no, boat, show |
| `ɪə` (UK) | here, near, idea |
| `eə` (UK) | hair, care, there |

Common issue: `oʊ`/`əʊ` in `go, no, home` said as a flat "o" (Hindi ओ). It should
**move**: start rounded-mid, glide toward "u". "Oh no, I don't know" – feel the slide.

## Practice routine (5 min/day, Week 2)

1. Pick **one** pair from the table above.
2. 10 minimal pairs – listen only (2 min).
3. Say 10 pairs, recorded (1 min).
4. One tongue-twister sentence, 10× (2 min):
   - `iː/ɪ`: "The cheap ship keeps sinking in the deep sea."
   - `uː/ʊ`: "The cook took a good look at the cool blue pool."
   - `æ`: "That fat cat had a bad hat."
   - `ɒ/ɔː`: "Paul saw a small ball fall on the wall."

Rotate a new pair every 2–3 days. Revisit ones that are still hard.
