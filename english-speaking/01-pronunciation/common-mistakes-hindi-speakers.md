# Common Pronunciation Mistakes for Hindi / Indian-English Speakers

These are the highest-impact fixes. If you only do this file, you still get 60% of
the benefit. For each: what happens, why, and the fix. Drill 2–3 at a time, not all.

Use **YouGlish** to hear each example word said by real speakers.

---

## 1. `v` and `w` are merged

Hindi has one sound (व) that sits between English `v` and `w`. So `vet`/`wet`,
`vine`/`wine`, `verse`/`worse` sound the same.

- **`v`** – top teeth touch bottom lip, voice on. Like `f` but buzzing. `van, very, seven, believe, love`.
- **`w`** – lips rounded and pushed forward, teeth touch nothing. `we, want, away, well`.

**Drill:** "We have very few visitors when the weather is wet."
Hold a finger lightly on your lips: for `w` they push against your finger; for `v`
they don't move but your lip vibrates on your teeth.

---

## 2. `th` becomes `t`/`d` (or `s`/`z`)

`think` → "tink", `this` → "dis", `three` → "tree".

- **Voiceless `th`** (`think, thanks, both, month, three, path`): tongue tip lightly
  between your teeth, blow air. No voice.
- **Voiced `th`** (`this, that, the, mother, breathe, with`): same tongue position,
  add voice.

**Drill:** put your tongue tip visibly between your teeth, say slowly:
"I think this thing is thirty-three." Exaggerate the tongue for a week.

---

## 3. Retroflex `t` and `d`

Hindi `t/d` are made with the tongue curled back (retroflex: ट/ड). English `t/d`
are made with the tongue tip on the **alveolar ridge** – the bump just behind your
top teeth – flatter and softer.

- Say `t` with the tongue further forward and lighter. `tea, time, water, better, city`.
- Between vowels in US English, `t` becomes a quick `d`-like tap: `water` ≈ "wader",
  `better` ≈ "bedder", `city` ≈ "sidy". (UK keeps a crisp `t`.)

## 4. Word stress on the wrong syllable

This wrecks comprehension more than any single sound. Indian English often stresses
earlier syllables or spreads stress evenly.

| Word | Common wrong | Correct (CAPS = stress) |
|------|--------------|-------------------------|
| development | DE-velopment | de-VEL-op-ment |
| hotel | HO-tel | ho-TEL |
| percent | PER-cent | per-CENT |
| available | a-VAIL-a-ble (ok) but often "avail-A-ble" | a-VAIL-a-ble |
| category | ca-TE-gory | CAT-e-gory |
| opportunity | oppor-TUN-ity (ok) | op-por-TU-ni-ty |
| colleague | col-LEAGUE | COL-league |
| purpose | pur-POSE | PUR-pose |
| develop | DE-velop | de-VEL-op |
| management | manage-MENT | MAN-age-ment |

→ Full method in [word-stress-and-rhythm.md](word-stress-and-rhythm.md).

## 5. Syllable-timed rhythm ("flat" / "fast" sound)

You give every syllable roughly equal length and volume. English speakers stretch
stressed syllables and crush the unstressed ones ("weak forms").

> "I would **like** to **talk** about the **pro**ject."
> The `to`, `the`, and `-ject` are short and quiet. `like`, `talk`, `pro` are long.

**Drill:** tap the table only on the stressed words while you read aloud. Let the
in-between words rush to catch up. → [word-stress-and-rhythm.md](word-stress-and-rhythm.md).

---

## 6. Extra vowel added to clusters and endings

`school` → "is-kool" / "suh-kool", `film` → "fil-um", `speak` → "is-peak".

- `sp-, st-, sk-, scr-, spr-` start with no vowel before the `s`. Just `s` straight
  into the next consonant. `speak, student, school, street`.
- Don't add "-uh" after final consonants: `film, help, milk, work, bird` end
  cleanly.

---

## 7. Missing or wrong vowel length (short vs long)

`ship`/`sheep`, `bit`/`beat`, `full`/`fool`, `cot`/`caught`, `pull`/`pool`.
Hindi speakers often use one mid vowel for both. → [vowel-sounds.md](vowel-sounds.md).

---

## 8. `-ed` endings

`-ed` has **three** sounds, never "ed" as a full syllable unless the word ends in
t/d:

- after voiceless sounds → `t`: `worked` = "workt", `stopped` = "stopt", `asked` = "askt".
- after voiced sounds → `d`: `played` = "playd", `called` = "calld", `used` = "yoozd".
- after `t`/`d` → `id`: `wanted` = "want-id", `needed` = "need-id", `decided` = "decide-id".

---

## 9. `-s` / plural and 3rd-person endings

- after voiceless → `s`: `books, cats, stops`.
- after voiced → `z`: `dogs, jobs, plays, comes, is, was, does` (the `s` buzzes).
- after s/z/sh/ch/j → `iz`: `boxes, watches, changes, buses`.

Many speakers drop these entirely ("he work here"). Keep them – they carry grammar.

---

## 10. `z` pronounced as `j` or `s`

`zoo`, `zero`, `zebra`, `easy`, `busy`, `these`, `prize`. It's a buzzing `s`, not
"joo" and not "soo". Also final `-se` in `because, please, rise, choose` is `z`.

---

## 11. `f` / `ph` sometimes → `p`

`coffee`, `office`, `photo`, `phone`, `film` – top teeth on bottom lip, blow air.
Not "poto" / "opis".

---

## 12. Stress-shift in noun vs verb pairs

Same spelling, stress moves:

| Noun (stress 1st) | Verb (stress 2nd) |
|---|---|
| a REcord | to re-CORD |
| a PREsent | to pre-SENT |
| an INcrease | to in-CREASE |
| a PROduce | to pro-DUCE |
| CONtent (things inside) | to be con-TENT (happy) |
| an OBject | to ob-JECT |
