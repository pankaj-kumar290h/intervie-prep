# 01 – Pronunciation

You do not need a "native accent." You need to be **clearly understood** and to
sound **natural enough** that people focus on your ideas, not your accent. That is
100% achievable for an adult learner.

## What actually matters (in order)

1. **Word stress** – putting the emphasis on the right syllable. Get this wrong and
   people genuinely cannot understand you, even if every sound is correct.
   → [word-stress-and-rhythm.md](word-stress-and-rhythm.md)
2. **Sentence rhythm** – English "eats" the small words and stresses the important
   ones. Indian English tends to give every syllable equal time (syllable-timed).
   English is stress-timed. This is the single biggest "accent" giveaway.
   → [word-stress-and-rhythm.md](word-stress-and-rhythm.md)
3. **A handful of specific sounds** that don't exist in Hindi or are swapped.
   → [common-mistakes-hindi-speakers.md](common-mistakes-hindi-speakers.md)
4. **Vowels** – English has ~15–20 vowel sounds; Hindi speakers often collapse them
   to ~5. `ship` vs `sheep`, `full` vs `fool`.
   → [vowel-sounds.md](vowel-sounds.md)
5. **Consonants** – `th`, `v/w`, light vs dark `l`, `z`, and not retroflexing `t/d`.
   → [consonant-sounds.md](consonant-sounds.md)
6. **Connected speech** – how words blend together at natural speed.
   → [connected-speech.md](connected-speech.md)
7. **Intonation** – the melody that signals question, statement, list, contrast,
   emotion.
   → [intonation.md](intonation.md)

## How to practice pronunciation

- **Minimal pairs** (ship/sheep): train your ear first, then your mouth.
  → [minimal-pairs-drills.md](minimal-pairs-drills.md)
- **Shadowing**: copy a native speaker in real time. This trains everything at once
  and is the highest-value drill. → [../04-speaking-practice/shadowing.md](../04-speaking-practice/shadowing.md)
- **Record and compare**: say a sentence, then play the original, then play
  yourself. The gap is your homework.
- **Exaggerate**: when drilling, over-do the target sound/stress. In real speech it
  will settle to normal.

## Tools

- **YouGlish** (youglish.com) – type any word, hear it said by dozens of real
  speakers in context. Best pronunciation tool that exists, and free.
- **Cambridge Dictionary** – every entry has UK + US audio and the phonetic
  spelling.
- **Your phone's voice recorder** – non-negotiable.
- Optional: **Elsa Speak** app – gives per-sound feedback on your speech.

## A realistic target

- Month 1: fix word stress on your 200 most-used words; fix `th`, `v/w`.
- Month 2: sentence rhythm feels less "flat"; vowel pairs mostly distinguished.
- Month 3: connected speech and intonation sound natural in prepared speech.
- Ongoing: it keeps improving for years. That is fine.

Pick the accent you hear most (US or UK) and stick to it so you're consistent.
Don't mix.
