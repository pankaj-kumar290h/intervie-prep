# Word Stress and Sentence Rhythm

**This is the most important file in the pronunciation folder.** Fixing stress and
rhythm does more for being understood than fixing any individual sound.

---

## Part 1 – Word stress

Every English word of 2+ syllables has **one** strongly stressed syllable. The
stressed syllable is: louder, longer, higher in pitch, and has a full clear vowel.
The others get reduced (often to schwa "uh").

`PHOtograph` → `phoTOGraphy` → `photoGRAPHic` – same root, stress moves, and the
vowels change with it.

### Why it matters
If you say `de-VE-lop-ment` as `DE-ve-lop-ment`, a listener's brain doesn't match it
to any word it knows. Wrong stress = wrong word.

### Patterns that help (not rules, tendencies)

| Pattern | Stress | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| 2-syllable nouns/adjectives | usually 1st | `TAble, HAPpy, PROblem, PROject (n), CONtract (n)` |
| 2-syllable verbs | often 2nd | `to preSENT, to reCORD, to deCIDE, to forGET` |
| words ending `-tion, -sion, -cian` | syllable **before** it | `informA-tion, deci-Sion, musi-Cian, educA-tion` |
| words ending `-ic` | syllable before it | `speCIfic, econOMic, fanTAStic, phoTOgraphic` |
| words ending `-ity` | syllable before it | `abIlity, secUrity, responsibIlity, comMUnity` |
| words ending `-ical, -ily, -ology, -graphy` | syllable before it | `LOGical, TEMporarily, biOLogy, geOGraphy` |
| words ending `-ate` (verbs) | often 1st or 2 back | `COMmunicate, PARticipate, apPREciate` |
| compound nouns | **1st** part | `KEYboard, LAPtop, WHITEhouse, BUS stop, SOFTware` |
| phrasal verbs | **2nd** part (the particle) | `to give UP, to look AFter, to find OUT` |
| noun/verb pairs | noun 1st, verb 2nd | `a REcord / to reCORD` (see common-mistakes file) |

### How to fix your personal list
1. List your 100–200 most-used words (job words, daily words).
2. Look each up on Cambridge Dictionary – it marks stress with `ˈ` before the
   stressed syllable.
3. Mark the stress in your notes. Say each 5× exaggerating the stressed syllable.
4. Re-record any word you got wrong until it's automatic.

## Part 2 – Sentence rhythm (stress-timed vs syllable-timed)

Hindi is **syllable-timed**: each syllable gets ~equal time. English is
**stress-timed**: the gap between two *stressed* syllables stays roughly constant,
so unstressed words get squeezed and sped up.

### Content words vs function words

- **Content words** (carry meaning, get stressed): nouns, main verbs, adjectives,
  adverbs, question words. → `DOG, RUN, HAPPY, QUICKLY, WHERE`
- **Function words** (grammar glue, unstressed, reduced): articles, prepositions,
  pronouns, auxiliaries, conjunctions, `to`, `that`. → `a, the, of, to, for, and,
  is, was, can, you, them`

> I could have **GONE** to the **PAR**ty but I **DE**cided to **STAY** at **HOME**.

Stressed: gone, par, de, stay, home. Everything else is quiet filler that rushes by.
"could have" → "could-uv" / "coulda". "to the" → "tuh thuh".

### Weak forms (function words in normal speech)

| Word | Strong | Weak (normal) |
|---|---|---|
| and | and | ən / n → "fish 'n chips" |
| to | too | tə → "I want tə go" |
| of | ov | əv → "cup əv tea" |
| for | for | fər → "this is fər you" |
| can | kæn | kən → "I kən help" (note: **can't** stays strong `kɑːnt`) |
| have | hæv | həv / əv → "I should əv known" |
| was | wɒz | wəz → "he wəz late" |
| are | ɑː | ər → "they're here" |
| you | yoo | yə → "d'yə know?" |
| your | yor | yər |
| at | æt | ət |
| do | doo | də → "whət də you mean?" |

Using weak forms is not "lazy" – it's **required** to sound natural. Over-clear
function words are the #1 giveaway of a learner.

### Drills

1. **Tap the stress.** Read a paragraph aloud. Tap the table only on stressed
   syllables. Keep the taps evenly spaced like a metronome – force the in-between
   words to speed up.
2. **Hum the sentence.** Before saying a sentence, hum its rhythm: "dut-dut-DAA-dut-
   dut-DAA-DUM". Then say it on that rhythm.
3. **Rubber-band sentences.** Same frame, growing middle, same total time:
   - "DOGS eat BONES."
   - "The DOGS eat the BONES."
   - "The DOGS will have eaten the BONES."
   Each version should take about the same time to say.
4. **Shadowing** trains this automatically – do it daily.
   → [../04-speaking-practice/shadowing.md](../04-speaking-practice/shadowing.md)

### Contrastive stress

You can move the stress to make a point:

- "**I** didn't say she stole it." (someone else did)
- "I didn't say **she** stole it." (someone else did)
- "I didn't say she **stole** it." (she borrowed it)

Practice saying that sentence 6 ways, stressing a different word each time.
