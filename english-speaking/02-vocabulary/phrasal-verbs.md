# Phrasal Verbs

A phrasal verb = verb + particle(s) with a meaning you often can't guess: `give up`,
`run into`, `look after`. Natural spoken English is *full* of them. Learners tend to
avoid them (using formal single verbs like "tolerate", "postpone", "investigate"),
which makes speech sound stiff and translated.

**Goal:** actively use ~100 common ones. Learn 3/day in Week 5, keep adding.

## How to learn them
- Learn with an object in a real sentence: not "put off" but "I put **the meeting**
  off till Friday."
- Note if it's **separable** (put the meeting off / put off the meeting – both ok;
  but with a pronoun it MUST split: "put **it** off", never "put off it").
- Note if it's **intransitive** (no object): "The car broke down."

## Daily life
- **wake up / get up** – "I woke up late and couldn't get up."
- **get dressed / get ready** – "Give me 10 minutes to get ready."
- **turn on / off / up / down** – "Can you turn the AC down?"
- **run out of** – "We've run out of milk."
- **throw away / throw out** – "I threw away the old boxes."
- **tidy up / clean up** – "I need to tidy up before guests come."
- **drop off / pick up** – "I'll drop you off and pick you up at 6."
- **eat out / order in** – "Let's eat out tonight."
- **go out / stay in** – "I'd rather stay in tonight."
- **show up / turn up** – "He didn't show up to the party."

## Work / projects
- **figure out / work out** – "I can't figure out why the test fails."
- **deal with / handle** – "I'll deal with the client."
- **come up with** – "She came up with a good idea."
- **carry out / go ahead with** – "We carried out the migration on Friday."
- **set up** – "I set up the CI pipeline."
- **take on** – "I took on too much this sprint."
- **put off / hold off on** – "Let's put the release off."
- **catch up (on) / fall behind** – "I need to catch up on emails."
- **go over / run through** – "Let's go over the plan."
- **point out** – "He pointed out a bug in my logic."
- **rule out** – "We ruled out a hardware issue."
- **narrow down** – "I narrowed it down to two options."
- **roll out / roll back** – "We rolled back the deploy."
- **hand over / take over** – "I handed the project over to Priya."
- **sign off on** – "The manager signed off on the budget."
- **loop in / fill in** – "Loop me in on that thread." / "Fill me in later."
- **get around to** – "I didn't get around to it."
- **keep up with** – "It's hard to keep up with all the changes."
- **wrap up** – "Let's wrap up the meeting."

## Communication / social
- **bring up** – "She brought up a good point."
- **get back to** – "I'll get back to you tomorrow."
- **catch up (with someone)** – "Let's catch up over coffee."
- **run into / bump into** – "I ran into an old colleague."
- **get along (with)** – "I get along well with my team."
- **fall out (with)** – "They fell out over money."
- **put up with** – "I can't put up with the noise."
- **look up to / look down on** – "I look up to my mentor."
- **back (someone) up** – "Thanks for backing me up in the meeting."
- **talk (someone) into / out of** – "He talked me into it."

## Change / progress
- **turn out** – "It turned out to be fine."
- **end up** – "I ended up staying late."
- **work out** (resolve) – "It all worked out in the end."
- **pick up** (improve/increase) – "Sales picked up."
- **slow down / speed up** – "Things slowed down after the launch."
- **come along / get on** (progress) – "How's the project coming along?"
- **give up / carry on / keep on** – "Don't give up." / "Carry on."
- **cut down on / cut back on** – "I'm cutting down on coffee."
- **take up** (a hobby) – "I took up running."
- **give up** (a habit) – "I gave up smoking."

## Understanding / problems
- **come across** (seem) – "He comes across as shy."
- **come across / run across** (find) – "I came across a useful library."
- **look into** – "I'll look into the issue."
- **sort out** – "We sorted out the bug."
- **mess up / screw up** – "I messed up the config."
- **mix up** – "I mixed up the two files."
- **break down** – "The build broke down." / "Negotiations broke down."
- **hold up** – "What's holding up the release?"
- **back up** (data) – "Back up your work."

## Separable vs not – quick reference

| Type | Example | With a pronoun |
|---|---|---|
| Separable | "turn **the light** off" or "turn off **the light**" | MUST split: "turn **it** off" ✅ / "turn off it" ❌ |
| Inseparable (transitive) | "look after **the kids**" | "look after **them**" ✅ (never "look them after") |
| Intransitive | "The plane **took off**." | no object |

When in doubt, split it if it *can* split and you're using a pronoun; otherwise keep
it together.

1. 3 phrasal verbs/day from above.
2. For each: say the given example, then 2 sentences about your own life, out loud.
3. Add to Anki with your sentence.
4. In your daily monologue, force in the day's 3.
5. Weekend: retell your week using only phrasal verbs where possible.
