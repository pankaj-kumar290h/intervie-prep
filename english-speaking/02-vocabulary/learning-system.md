# The Vocabulary Learning System

A word is "learned" when you can produce it, correctly and fast, while speaking
about something else. Recognition ≠ knowing. Here's the pipeline to get there.

---

## Step 1 – Capture (during the day)

Keep **one** place – a phone note or a small notebook – called `Phrases`.
Whenever you:
- hear/read a phrase you'd like to be able to say, **or**
- want to say something in a conversation and can't,

write it down. One line. Don't stop your day to study it.

Example capture:
```
- to be swamped = very busy   (heard on podcast: "I'm swamped this week")
- ??? how to say "I got confused between X and Y"  → (look up)
```

---

## Step 2 – Process (once a day, ~10 min)

Go through the day's captures. For each keeper, make a **card** (Anki or a table):

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| Chunk | `to be swamped (with work)` |
| Meaning | extremely busy, overloaded |
| Example (real) | "Sorry for the late reply, I've been swamped this week." |
| My sentence | "I was swamped with bug reports after the release." |
| Sound note | stress: sw**amped** |

**Rules:**
- Store the **chunk / collocation**, not the bare word.
- Write **your own** example about **your** life. This is the memory hook.
- Max 5–10 cards/day. Drop the weak captures without guilt.

---

## Step 3 – Activate (same day, out loud)

Say your sentence for each new chunk **3 times, out loud**. Then, in your daily
self-talk / monologue, deliberately force in 3 of today's chunks. Clumsy is fine.

> "Today I was kind of *swamped*. I had to *deal with* a lot of *back-to-back*
> meetings, so I didn't *get around to* the code review."

---

## Step 4 – Review (spaced repetition)

- **With Anki:** just do your daily reviews. It schedules 1d / 3d / 1w / 2w / 1m...
  automatically. ~5–10 min/day. This is the recommended path.
- **Without Anki (paper):** keep cards in a box with dividers: "Today, +1 day, +3
  days, +1 week, +2 weeks". Each day, review the due divider; move a card forward if
  you got it, back to start if you didn't. (This is the "Leitner system".)

Review = cover the meaning/example, see the chunk, say your sentence aloud. If it
doesn't come in ~5 seconds, it's a fail – reset it.

---

## Step 5 – Retire

When a chunk comes out naturally in real conversation 2–3 times, it's yours. Anki
will space it to months, then you can suspend it. Done.

---

## Anki setup (5 minutes, one time)

1. Install Anki (desktop, free) + AnkiDroid (Android, free) or AnkiMobile (iOS,
   paid). Sync via a free AnkiWeb account.
2. Create a deck: `English – Chunks`.
3. Card type: **Basic (and reversed)** is overkill; use **Basic**.
   - Front: the chunk (+ optionally a gap sentence: "I've been ___ all week.")
   - Back: meaning + real example + your sentence.
4. Add TTS or a sound if you want pronunciation practice (optional).
5. Settings: new cards/day = 10, max reviews/day = 100.

Do reviews every day, even 2 minutes. Missing days makes the pile scary.

---

## What NOT to do

- ❌ Copying long word lists into Anki without examples – you'll bury yourself.
- ❌ Learning words you'll never say ("perspicacious"). Learn what *you* need.
- ❌ Translating word-for-word from Hindi. Learn the English chunk as its own unit.
- ❌ Only reading the cards. **Say the sentence out loud** every review.
- ❌ Adding 40 cards after one movie. Cap it. The backlog kills the habit.

---

## Minimal version (if Anki feels like too much)

A running Google Doc / notebook with a table (chunk | meaning | my sentence).
Every Sunday, read the whole thing aloud, saying each "my sentence". Star the ones
that were hard; those get read first next week. Low-tech, still works.
