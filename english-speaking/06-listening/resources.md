# 06 – Listening (Input)

You cannot produce sounds and rhythms you haven't absorbed. Listening is the fuel.
Aim for **20–30 minutes of English audio a day**, most of it enjoyable, plus your
focused shadowing clip.

---

## Two kinds of listening – do both

### 1. Extensive listening (relaxed, for volume)
Podcasts / shows you enjoy, ~90% understandable, no stopping. On your commute, while
cooking, at the gym. The goal is *hours of exposure*, not study. This builds
intuition for grammar, collocations, and rhythm in the background.

### 2. Intensive listening (focused, for detail)
A short clip (1–3 min), with transcript. Listen repeatedly. Catch every word. Mark
reductions and linking. This is where you feed your shadowing practice.
→ [../04-speaking-practice/shadowing.md](../04-speaking-practice/shadowing.md)

---

## Choosing your ONE main source

Pick **one** show or podcast for the first month and stay with it. Familiarity with
voices, topic, and vocabulary compounds fast. Criteria:
- You genuinely enjoy it (you'll actually listen).
- Natural conversational speech (not scripted news-reading).
- Transcripts or accurate subtitles available.
- The accent you want (pick US or UK and be consistent).

---

## Suggestions by level

### Building up (clear speech, made for learners)
- **All Ears English** / **The English We Speak (BBC)** – short, conversational.
- **Culips ESL Podcast**, **RealLife English** – natural speech, explained.
- **Learn English with... (TED-Ed / VOA Learning English)** – slower, transcript.
- Any "English learners" podcast on Spotify with transcripts.

### Real content, still accessible
- **Conversational podcasts** on a topic you like (tech: *Syntax*, *Lex Fridman*
  (slow, clear), *Changelog*; general: *The Daily*, *Search Engine*, *99% Invisible*).
- **YouTube:** creators who talk to camera in a clear, natural way. Tech: Fireship
  (fast!), Theo, ThePrimeagen (fast), Kevin Powell. Turn on English captions.
- **Sitcoms** with simple dialogue: *Friends*, *The Office (US)*, *Brooklyn
  Nine-Nine*, *Ted Lasso*. Rewatch episodes you know – you'll catch more each time.

### Stretch (fast, native, idiomatic)
- Talk shows, stand-up comedy, unscripted interviews, movies without subtitles.
- Tech conference talks at full speed.

### Interview-specific
- Mock interview videos on YouTube ("software engineer mock interview",
  "behavioral interview example answers").
- **Exponent**, **Hello Interview** channels – people explaining technical things
  out loud, which is exactly the skill you're building.

---

## How to use subtitles

| Stage | Setting |
|---|---|
| Struggling with a show | English subtitles ON |
| Comfortable | English subtitles ON, glance only when lost |
| Confident | subtitles OFF for the first watch, ON for a rewatch |

**Never** use subtitles in your own language for training – that keeps you in
translation mode. → [../05-fluency-confidence/thinking-in-english.md](../05-fluency-confidence/thinking-in-english.md)

---

## Active-listening micro-habits

While doing extensive listening, occasionally:
- **Repeat the last phrase** the speaker said, out loud, copying their tone (mini-
  shadowing).
- **Predict** the next word before they say it.
- **Notice one thing:** "that's how they say 'schedule'", "he linked those two
  words", "she stressed 'actually'".
- **Steal a phrase:** hear something useful → say it out loud → add to your phrase
  notebook → use it that day.

---

## Daily listening plan

| Slot | Type | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Commute / chores | Extensive – your main podcast/show | 15–25 min |
| Study block | Intensive – shadowing clip | 6 min (part of Daily Core) |
| Optional wind-down | A show episode with English subs | 20 min |

Total enjoyable exposure: 30–50 min/day with almost no extra effort.
